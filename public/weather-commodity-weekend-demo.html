How the full research app extends this demo

Weather evidence, with provenance and limits

The AlphaForge/Astraeus research app compares multi-model forecasts with historical climate conditions. Its crop screen includes a 30-day precipitation-minus-reference-ET₀ anomaly and a depth-weighted ERA5 soil-moisture anomaly for the latest complete week.

Important distinction

Context is not a forecast of the market

  • ERA5 soil moisture is a lagged reanalysis estimate, not a field measurement or future forecast.
  • Regional sample points are not production-weighted crop maps.
  • Weather signals do not establish yield loss or causally explain a price move.
  • Price prediction requires point-in-time fundamentals and walk-forward validation.

Weekend project: connecting weather patterns to potential commodity price pressure 🌦️🌾 I built a research prototype to explore how regional weather conditions can translate into physical supply risks—and what evidence is needed before connecting those risks to commodity markets. The full app combines multi-model weather outlooks with historical climate context. For crop regions, I added two complementary signals: • A 30-day precipitation-minus-reference-evapotranspiration measure • A depth-weighted ERA5 soil-moisture anomaly for the latest complete week One represents recent atmospheric water balance; the other is a lagged reanalysis estimate—not a field measurement or a forecast of future soil moisture. The goal is to make the reasoning traceable: weather anomaly → possible crop or supply disruption → conditional price-pressure hypothesis. Important caveat: this prototype does not prove that weather caused a price move, estimate crop losses, or provide a validated commodity-price forecast. Inventories, alternative origins, policy, crop stage and market expectations can change or offset the transmission. Next step: validate regional signals against observed soil moisture, crop reports and inventory data, then test market hypotheses out of sample against simple baselines. Built with TypeScript and open weather data. Feedback on validation methods and useful agricultural datasets is welcome.
